# Climate Science: Urban Heat Islands (UHI)

## Definition & Overview
An Urban Heat Island (UHI) is a microclimatic phenomenon in which urban areas experience significantly warmer temperatures than surrounding rural or vegetated hinterlands. UHIs manifest in both atmospheric air temperature (Canopy Layer UHI, measured by 2m sensors) and surface temperature (Surface UHI, measured by satellite radiometry such as MODIS LST).

## Contributing Physical Drivers
1. **Thermal Mass & Reduced Albedo**: Concrete, asphalt, and masonry possess high volumetric heat capacities and lower albedos (absorbing 85–95% of incoming solar radiation), storing heat during daylight and reradiating it slowly through the night.
2. **Loss of Vegetative Evapotranspiration**: Replacement of natural vegetation with impervious surfaces eliminates evaporative latent heat cooling, shifting the Bowen ratio ($\beta = H / \lambda E$) heavily toward sensible heating.
3. **Urban Canyon Geometry**: Tall buildings trap outgoing longwave radiation (reduced Sky View Factor), reducing nighttime radiative cooling to space.
4. **Anthropogenic Heat Flux ($Q_F$)**: Waste heat from vehicles, air conditioning condensers, power generators, and industrial processes directly heats the urban canopy layer.

## Bengaluru & Rapidly Urbanizing Metropolises
Bengaluru, Karnataka (Latitude 12.9716°N, Longitude 77.5946°E), historically known as India's "Garden City," provides a quintessential case study in rapid urbanization and microclimatic transformation. Over the last four decades (1980s–2020s), rapid expansion of IT corridors, infrastructure development, and substantial reduction in tree canopy cover and water bodies (lakes) have demonstrably intensified the urban heat island effect, detectable in multi-decadal NASA POWER and MODIS thermal time-series.
