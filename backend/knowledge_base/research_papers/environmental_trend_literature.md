# Scientific Literature: Regional Environmental Trends & Case Studies

## 1. Indian Subcontinent & Karnataka Climatological Trends
- **Reference**: Pai, D. S., et al. (2014). "Analysis of temperature and rainfall trends over India." *Mausam*, 65(1), 1-18.
- **Key Finding**: Over peninsular India, mean annual surface temperatures have risen at approximately $+0.5^\circ\text{C}$ to $+0.7^\circ\text{C}$ per century, with acceleration observed in post-1990 decades. Urbanizing cities (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai) show accelerated minimum temperature warming rates ($+0.25^\circ\text{C}$ to $+0.35^\circ\text{C}$ per decade) driven by urban canopy heat trapping.

## 2. Global Terrestrial Wind Stilling & Reversal
- **Reference**: Zeng, Z., et al. (2019). "A reversal in global terrestrial stilling for wind speed." *Nature Climate Change*, 9, 979-985.
- **Key Finding**: Analysis of surface anemometer networks and MERRA-2 reanalysis confirmed that terrestrial wind speeds declined at roughly $-0.08 \text{ m/s}$ per decade between 1978 and 2010 ("stilling"), but reversed to an upward recovery post-2010 driven by decadal ocean-atmosphere oscillations (Pacific Decadal Oscillation and North Atlantic Oscillation).

## 3. Global Precipitation Extremes & Thermodynamic Scaling
- **Reference**: Donat, M. G., et al. (2016). "More extreme precipitation in the world's dry and wet regions." *Nature Climate Change*, 6, 508-513.
- **Key Finding**: Observations confirm that daily precipitation extremes have intensified globally across both wet and dry climate regimes at rates consistent with the Clausius-Clapeyron thermodynamic scaling ($\approx 6-7\% \text{ per } ^\circ\text{C}$).
