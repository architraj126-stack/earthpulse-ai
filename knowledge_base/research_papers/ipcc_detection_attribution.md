# Research Paper Synthesis: IPCC Framework for Trend Detection & Attribution

## Reference Citation
Hegerl, G. C., et al. (2010). "Good Practice Guidance Paper on Detection and Attribution Related to Anthropogenic Climate Change." In: Meeting Report of the IPCC Expert Meeting on Detection and Attribution Related to Anthropogenic Climate Change.

## Conceptual Architecture
1. **Trend Detection**:
   - The process of demonstrating that an observed change in an environmental variable is statistically significantly different from what can be explained by natural internal climate variability alone.
   - Requires long observational baseline (typically $\ge 30$ years) to distinguish multi-decadal trends from low-frequency modes (AMO, PDO, ENSO).
2. **Attribution**:
   - The process of establishing the principal causes of the detected trend with an assigned degree of statistical confidence.
   - Uses optimal fingerprinting techniques: projecting observational space-time patterns onto modeled responses to specific forcings (Greenhouse gases, aerosols, solar irradiance, volcanic eruptions).
3. **Implications for EarthPulse AI Users**:
   - When EarthPulse AI detects a statistically significant trend (e.g., $p < 0.01$ warming in Bengaluru), the platform confirms **Detection**.
   - The AI assistant explicitly contextualizes **Attribution** by explaining how urban development and global greenhouse forcing both contribute, avoiding oversimplified single-cause assertions.
